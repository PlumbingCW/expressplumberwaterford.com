When a snake is not enough

Emergency hydro jetting in Waterford, CT is for blockages that a cable cannot fully clear. If your main line was snaked and clogged again a few weeks later, the pipe walls may be coated with grease or scale, or roots may be growing back through a joint.

Hydro jetting uses a hose and a nozzle that sends high-pressure water in all directions, scouring the pipe wall instead of punching a hole through a clog. We run a camera first so we know the pipe is sound enough to jet.

A main drain backing up: act now

A backed-up main line can push wastewater into the lowest drain in the house. That is a health hazard and a damage risk.

Signs your line needs jetting instead of snaking

A cable machine is good at opening a hole through a blockage. It is less effective at removing the grease and scale that line the pipe wall. If the line drains for a short time and then slows again, a coating is probably still there.

Another sign is a slow main line across several fixtures, especially after heavy use on weekends. Recurring odors or gurgling from floor drains can also point to buildup in the lower run.

Roots need a different step. A jetter with the right nozzle can cut and flush fine roots, but heavy root masses or a broken pipe may need sewer line repair instead.

Jetting versus snaking

Pick the tool that fits the pipe and the blockage.

ToolBest forLimits
Cable machineOpening a clog quicklyCan leave a coating on the pipe wall.
Hydro jettingGrease, scale, silt and fine rootsNeeds a sound pipe and a reachable cleanout.
Camera inspectionFinding the cause and checking pipe conditionShows the problem but does not clear it.

How a jetting visit goes

We follow a fixed order to protect your pipe.

  1. Locate the cleanout and confirm the line material.
  2. Run a camera to see the blockage and check for cracks or sags.
  3. Choose the nozzle and pressure that match the pipe.
  4. Jet from the cleanout toward the street or tank, then pull back to flush.
  5. Re-run the camera so you can see the clean pipe on the monitor.

Waterford conditions worth knowing

Kitchens along a commercial corridor such as Boston Post Road put grease into drain lines, so a commercial line may need jetting on a schedule. Our commercial plumbing team can build a maintenance plan.

Waterford homes on septic systems need a different approach. Jetting a septic outlet line is possible, but a full tank, a clogged baffle or a failed leach field are separate problems. Tell the dispatcher which system you have.

After the pipe is clean

A freshly jetted line stays clean longer if the cause is addressed. Kitchen grease should go in a container instead of the sink, and flushable wipes should not go down the toilet at all. A yard tree with roots near the line may need a root-control plan or an eventual repair.

If the camera showed a belly, a cracked joint or an offset section, we will explain the repair options in plain terms. A recurring problem after jetting almost always points to a physical defect in the pipe, not poor cleaning.

Code and permit note

Cleaning a line does not change the plumbing, so it generally does not need a permit. If a camera shows damage and a repair follows, the Connecticut State Building Code adopts the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), and the Waterford Building Department states that permits are required before improvements.

What to do before a jetting visit

Make the visit safe and short.

  • Clear the area around the cleanout, indoors or out.
  • Keep pets and children away from the work area.
  • Stop running water into the affected drains.
  • Tell the plumber about any past repairs or known damage.
  • Move stored items away from floor drains.

What changes the price

Jetting price depends on the line and the work involved.

  • Line length and diameter.
  • Cleanout access, or whether one must be installed.
  • Camera inspection before and after.
  • The type and amount of buildup or root growth.
  • Emergency timing, since overnight dispatch is a true emergency call.
  • Insurance note: a slow buildup is generally maintenance, not a covered event, so check your policy.
