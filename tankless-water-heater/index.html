Tankless water heaters in Waterford homes

A tankless water heater heats water on demand instead of storing it, so it does not run out during a long shower. It also needs more careful venting, gas line sizing and regular maintenance than a tank, which is why a plan matters before you buy.

We install tankless units, repair error codes and perform descaling flushes. If your tankless unit is not heating, a quick call can tell you whether it is a service issue or something larger.

Is tankless right for your home?

Tankless works well for households that need steady hot water for back-to-back showers or for homes where floor space is tight. It tends to disappoint where the gas line is undersized, the vent run is awkward or the incoming water is very cold in winter and flow demand is high.

Southeastern Connecticut winters cool the incoming water, so a tankless unit has to raise the temperature further than it would in a mild climate. A unit sized for a hot climate can feel weak here, so size by flow rate and temperature rise.

If you have used a standard tank for years, ask us about installation options before deciding. A heat pump tank can be a better fit than tankless in some basements.

Why descaling matters

Mineral scale builds on the heat exchanger and shrinks the water passages. The unit may show an error code, deliver lukewarm water or shut off early. A flush with a pump, hoses and a descaling solution restores flow and protects the exchanger.

Public water supplied under the City of New London Utilities report is not listed there with a hardness figure, so we cannot give a hardness number for Waterford. Test your own tap, or follow the state health department's guidance to test a private well. If hardness is high, a more frequent flush may be worth it.

Common tankless problems we fix

Many calls follow the same few patterns.

  • Error codes for ignition, flame or exhaust faults.
  • Cold water sandwich, where hot water briefly turns cold between uses.
  • Low flow from a clogged inlet screen or scaled exchanger.
  • Condensate drain blockage on high-efficiency models.
  • Gas supply or vent problems after a nearby remodel.
  • Insurance note: tankless repairs after a sudden leak may fall under water damage coverage, but wear and scale buildup usually do not.

Sizing a tankless unit for a Connecticut winter

Two numbers set the right size: how many gallons per minute you need at peak and how many degrees the water must rise. A family running a shower and a dishwasher together may need a larger unit than the same family showering alone.

If you are comparing models, ask for the flow rate at your expected temperature rise instead of the headline maximum. That is the figure that predicts whether the last person in the morning gets a hot shower.

Code and permit note

Tankless units add gas sizing, venting and condensate rules to the usual plumbing code. The Connecticut building code adopts the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC) for plumbing, and gas appliance work follows its own fuel gas rules. Ask the Building Department how your install will be permitted.

Installing in Connecticut

A licensed plumber must handle the gas and water connections. The Connecticut Department of Consumer Protection issues plumbing licenses, and homeowners can search a name on elicense.ct.gov before hiring.

Ask the Waterford Building Department, what permits apply to your install. Tell them the fuel type and vent route so they can answer accurately.
