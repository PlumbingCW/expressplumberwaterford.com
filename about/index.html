About Waterford Express Plumbing

Who answers the phone

Waterford Express Plumbing is a 24/7 plumbing line for Waterford, Connecticut and the towns around it. When you call, a dispatcher asks what is happening and where, gives you steps to limit damage and sends a licensed plumber when one is available.

We do not list personal names or license numbers on this site, because the plumber who responds depends on the time and the job. You can check any plumber's Connecticut license on the Department of Consumer Protection eLicense lookup, and you should ask to see identification when someone arrives.

That approach keeps the site honest. Every claim on these pages is something we can stand behind on the phone, and anything we cannot promise, such as an exact arrival time, we leave out.

How a call works from start to finish

The steps below are the same for a burst pipe at midnight and a slow drain on a Tuesday afternoon, though the speed changes.

  1. You call the line and describe the problem and the address.
  2. The dispatcher gives you safety steps, such as closing the main valve.
  3. A licensed plumber is sent and you get a current arrival window.
  4. The plumber finds the cause and explains the likely price before work starts.
  5. The repair is finished, tested and explained, with notes on what to watch.
  6. You receive an invoice that matches what was discussed.

How we think about price

Plumbing prices depend on the cause, the parts, the access and the time of day. We explain those factors on every service page, so you know what moves a price before you call.

If a job grows, the plumber tells you why before changing the price. If a repair is not worth the cost, we say so and describe the alternatives, including replacement.

What we point you to

Plumbing sits alongside several local and state offices, and we send you to the right one when a problem is not ours to fix.

  • The Waterford Utility Commission, for water meters, street side leaks and service line questions.
  • New London Public Utilities, whose annual report covers the water supplied to Waterford.
  • The Waterford Building Department, for permits on larger plumbing work.
  • The Connecticut Department of Consumer Protection, to check a plumber's license.
  • Your insurer, for claims after sudden water damage.

What we do not do

We do not make promises about arrival times that we cannot keep, and we do not claim reviews or awards that we cannot prove. We do not use online forms, so you always reach a person. Calls to our line may be recorded for quality and training purposes.

What to expect when the plumber arrives

The plumber introduces themselves, shows identification on request and asks you to point out the problem. They look at the cause before touching anything, then explain what they found in plain language.

Expect a short walk through the options. A quick repair, a larger repair and a replacement each have a place, and the plumber should tell you which one fits your situation and why. You decide, and nothing starts until you agree to the price.

After the work, the plumber tests the repair, cleans the work area and explains what to watch over the next few days. If a follow up is needed, such as a permit inspection or a second visit for parts, you hear it before the plumber leaves.

How to prepare before a visit

A little preparation speeds up the visit and keeps your home safe.

  • Clear a path to the problem, such as the area under a sink or around the water heater.
  • Know where your main water shutoff is and whether it turns.
  • Write down when the problem started and anything you tried.
  • Photograph any damage for your insurer.
  • Keep pets away from the work area.

Where we work

Our plumbers serve Waterford, Quaker Hill, Niantic, East Lyme, New London and Montville. See the full list of services to find the one that fits your problem.

Waterford sits on Long Island Sound and covers about 44.6 square miles, with the Niantic River along its western edge. That mix of shoreline, older villages and commercial corridors is why we ask about your address and the age of the home on the call. Older homes tend to hide more surprises behind walls, and a short description of the house helps the plumber bring the right parts on the first trip.

The same questions help us decide whether a visit should be treated as an emergency or scheduled for the next available morning. Either way, you hear the plan before anyone drives to your door, and you can change your mind at any time before work starts.
