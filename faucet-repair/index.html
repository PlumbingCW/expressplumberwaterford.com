Stopping the drip and the leak

Emergency faucet repair in Waterford, CT is for leaks you cannot ignore. Examples are a faucet that will not shut off, a supply line spraying under the sink or a shutoff valve that fails when you close it. Call the 24/7 line and we will walk you through the first step.

A steady drip is not an emergency, but it wastes water and often grows worse. A drip usually means a worn washer, cartridge or seat, and the fix is much cheaper when caught early.

Water that will not stop

A fixture that cannot be shut off needs the supply closed upstream until the plumber arrives.

Reading the drip

A faucet that drips from the spout has a worn cartridge, washer or seat. A faucet that leaks from the handle has a failed O-ring or packing. A leak at the base is usually a loose or damaged seal.

Single-handle faucets with a cartridge are quick to repair when parts are available. Older faucets with discontinued parts may be better replaced, and we say so honestly if repair costs approach replacement.

Under the sink, a leaking supply line or a failed compression fitting can cause damage for weeks before it is noticed. A flashlight and a dry paper towel run along each connection will show a slow leak.

Tub and shower valves are a related repair. If the valve will not shut off completely, the issue may be inside the wall, and the work is more involved.

Faucet or valve leaking?

Call the 24/7 line. If the leak is behind a wall, ask about leak detection, and if you are remodeling, see plumbing installation.

What changes the price

Faucet repairs vary by fixture and access.

  • Brand and parts availability.
  • Whether the shutoff valves work.
  • Access under a vanity or behind a tub wall.
  • Faucet type, kitchen, bath or tub and shower.
  • After-hours dispatch for a fixture that cannot be shut off.
  • Insurance note: sudden supply-line failures may be covered, while a drip over time is typically not.

Small habits that extend faucet life

Shut off the water under a sink before a long trip, and replace any rubber supply line that is more than a few years old. Braided lines fail less often but still age.

Turn handles gently. Over-tightening the handle on a washer-style faucet wears the seat and makes the drip return sooner. If a handle needs force, the cartridge is telling you it is worn.

Repair or replace a faucet

A drip from the spout is usually a worn cartridge or seat and is worth repairing. A faucet with a corroded body, a cracked handle or no available parts is better replaced.

If you are updating a kitchen or bath, tell us the hole count in the sink or counter. That decides which faucets fit without new cutting.

Common faucet symptoms

These point to the likely cause.

  • Drip from the spout: worn cartridge, washer or seat.
  • Leak at the base of the handle: worn O-ring or packing.
  • Low flow: clogged aerator or failing supply line.
  • Handle hard to turn: mineral buildup in the cartridge.
  • Water under the sink: supply line or shutoff valve.

Code and permit note

Swapping a faucet is minor work, but the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), adopted by the Connecticut State Building Code, sets rules on backflow protection and water temperature at fixtures. Anti scald and air gap details are part of why we match parts carefully.

What to do when a faucet will not shut off

A few steps stop the water fast.

  • Close the shutoff valves under the sink.
  • If they will not turn, close the main water valve.
  • Place a bucket or towels to protect the cabinet.
  • Do not force the handle further.
  • Call the 24/7 line before the leak spreads.

Fixtures and water in Waterford

Coastal homes near Great Neck and Mago Point deal with salt air, which can corrode exposed fixtures. Choosing quality finishes and checking hose bibs each season helps.

Public water supplied under the City of New London Utilities report is treated with phosphate for corrosion control. Fixtures can still collect mineral scale, and a vinegar soak on an aerator helps. If you use a private well, the state health department recommends annual testing, which also shows if hardness is a factor.
