What the city's water report says

New London Public Utilities draws water from reservoirs in a protected watershed that spans Waterford, Montville and Salem. The largest reservoir, Lake Konomoc, holds more than a billion gallons. Phosphate is added to the finished water to control corrosion, and lime is added to adjust pH.

Lead and copper sampling reported for 2023 stayed well under the action levels. That is reassuring about the water leaving the plant, but it does not tell you what your own service line and indoor pipes are made of. The city maintains a service line material inventory that you can check for your address.

If your house has older galvanized or lead pipe, test results at the plant do not protect you from what happens inside your walls. A plumber can check the pipe at the meter and advise on replacement. See repiping for the options.

Sodium was measured at about 10 ppm in the same report, so the water is not a concern for most diets, and the report advises people on sodium restricted diets to ask a physician.

Older housing and drains

In a city with older houses, drain lines often run through original piping and join lines that have settled over time. A slow tub, a gurgling toilet or a damp floor near the lowest drain is an early warning. Catching it early is far cheaper than a backup on a weekend.

We clear the line, then run a camera when the problem keeps returning. See drain cleaning and sewer inspection for what each visit involves.

For restaurants and shops downtown, we handle commercial plumbing calls and schedule noisy work for quiet hours when the problem allows it.

Permits in the city

New London's Building Division is at 111 Union Street. Its permit applications ask for a current contractor license and workers compensation certificate when a contractor does the work, and the city does not accept emailed applications.

We bring those documents to a job that needs a permit, and we tell you early when a job is likely to need one.

Code reference for New London

New London's Building Division applies the Connecticut State Building Code, which adopts the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC) according to the Office of the State Building Inspector. Sewer and drain repairs in older city buildings are where that standard matters most, and we plan the work around it.
