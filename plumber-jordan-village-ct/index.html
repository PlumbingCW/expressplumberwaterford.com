Waterford's original settlement

Jordan Village is Waterford's original settlement. The Jordan Village Historic District, centered where North Road and Avery Lane meet Rope Ferry Road, was listed on the National Register of Historic Places on August 23, 1990. The area lies in the Jordan Brook watershed.

It is a mixed area of older homes, civic buildings and small offices. Call with your address and what you need, and we will explain how the work would go.

The village and the civic area beside it

Jordan Green is the site of the Jordan Schoolhouse, the earliest recorded schoolhouse in town at 1737, which is now a museum. Next to the village, the town's planning documents describe a Civic Triangle that holds Town Hall, the library and the police station.

The town's 1998 plan also shows multifamily housing at Jordan Commons. A mix of single homes, multifamily buildings and small offices means the right plumbing question depends on the building, so we ask what you have.

Historic buildings and plumbing work

Many buildings in a historic village were built long before modern fixtures. Whether yours has older pipes depends on the building and any updates over the years, so a plumber has to look rather than assume.

If you plan work that changes a historic building, ask the Building Department what applies before you start. For a planned upgrade, see plumbing installation, and for old pipes that keep leaking, see repiping.

Code reference

According to the Connecticut Office of the State Building Inspector, the 2022 Connecticut State Building Code adopts the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC) for permit applications filed from October 1, 2022. A repair in this part of Waterford is judged by the same code as one anywhere else in the state.
