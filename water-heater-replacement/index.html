Replacing a failed water heater in Waterford

Emergency water heater replacement in Waterford, CT is a same-day job when equipment is available. When a tank leaks from the body, repair rarely makes sense, and the practical goal is to get hot water back and protect the floor around it.

A good replacement is more than lifting one tank out and another in. The new unit needs the right capacity, a safe flue or electrical connection, a relief valve piped to a safe spot and water connections that match the old layout.

Tank failed overnight: your first steps

A failed tank can release dozens of gallons before anyone notices. Act quickly to limit the damage.

Choosing the right size and type

Tank capacity should match your household's peak hot water use, not just the number of bathrooms. A family that runs showers, a dishwasher and laundry in the same hour needs more recovery capacity than the label alone suggests.

Standard tank heaters remain the usual emergency replacement because they fit existing piping. Heat pump models and tankless units are worth discussing, though they often need extra electrical, drain or venting work that a same-day swap may not allow.

The federal 25C home improvement credit for water heaters ended for equipment placed in service after December 31, 2025. Check Energize CT and your electric utility for any current rebates before you choose a heat pump model.

What is included in a replacement

Scope is stated before we start so there are no surprises.

  • Shutting off and draining the old tank, then removing and hauling it away.
  • New flexible connectors and a new relief valve and discharge pipe where required.
  • Gas or electric connection checked, with vent and combustion air reviewed on gas models.
  • Expansion tank check if your system has a closed supply.
  • Filling, purging air, testing for leaks and setting a safe temperature.
  • Insurance note: ask your insurer whether water damage from a sudden failure is covered, and keep the old unit's tag if they ask for it.

Keeping the old unit from happening again

Most tanks fail from the inside, where the steel liner wears through after years of heating cycles. The anode rod is the part that sacrifices itself to protect the liner, and checking it every few years can stretch a tank's life.

After a replacement, we show you the shutoff valves, the relief valve and the drain, so you know where to go next time. Write the install date on the tank in marker. It makes the next decision much easier.

Code and permit note

A replacement is regulated work. According to the Connecticut Office of the State Building Inspector, the 2022 state building code adopts the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), and a new heater has to meet its rules for relief valves, pan drains and venting. Ask the Waterford Building Department how it handles the permit.

Permits, licensing and local rules

Plumbing and gas work are licensed trades in Connecticut. The Department of Consumer Protection issues the licenses, including P-1 for unlimited plumbing contractors and P-2 for journeypersons, and you can verify any plumber on the state's eLicense lookup at elicense.ct.gov.

The Waterford Building Department at 15 Rope Ferry Road states that building permits are required before new construction, additions, renovations or improvements. Permit rules for a like-for-like water heater swap vary, so ask the building department what applies to your address.
