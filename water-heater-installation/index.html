Installing a water heater the right way

A planned water heater installation in Waterford, CT gives you time to pick the right type, size and location before anything fails. The work is straightforward when it is planned, and it avoids the rushed choices that come with a flooded basement.

If your current heater has already failed, see water heater replacement. This page covers new installs, upgrades, moves and switching fuel types.

Where the heater goes matters

Location affects efficiency, safety and service access. A heater in a basement near a floor drain is easier to protect from leaks than one over a finished room. Gas units need combustion air and a vent path to the outdoors that meets code.

Heat pump water heaters pull heat from the surrounding air, so they work best in a space with room to breathe and a condensate drain nearby. They also cool the room slightly, which many people welcome in a basement.

A new install is the best time to add a drain pan with a piped drain, a service shutoff valve on each line and a union connection that makes future replacement quicker.

Our installation checklist

Every install follows the same safety checklist.

  • Confirm capacity from your household use and fixture count.
  • Install a temperature and pressure relief valve with a full-size discharge pipe.
  • Fit an expansion tank when the supply has a backflow device or pressure regulator.
  • Check gas pressure, venting and combustion air on gas units.
  • Label the shutoff valves and show you where they are.
  • Test for leaks and flush air from the lines before handoff.

Permits and licensed work in Connecticut

Connecticut licenses plumbers through the Department of Consumer Protection. P-1 is the unlimited plumbing contractor license and P-2 is the unlimited journeyperson license, and any homeowner can check a license on the eLicense lookup at elicense.ct.gov.

Waterford's Building Department at 15 Rope Ferry Road takes permit applications in person or through the CITY SQUARED portal. It states that permits are required before improvements, so call to confirm what your install needs.

What to have ready before the plumber arrives

Clear a path from the door to the heater and move stored items at least a few feet back. Note whether you have a gas meter, a spare breaker slot or a floor drain close by, since those details change the plan and the price.

If you rent or manage a property, tell us who approves the work. Landlords and property managers often want the model, the fuel type and the schedule in writing, and we can provide that so the visit goes smoothly.

Code and permit note

A new installation brings code questions: where the relief pipe ends, how the vent is routed and whether an expansion tank is needed. The state building code adopts the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), and the Waterford Building Department decides what your project needs. We plan with those rules in mind.
