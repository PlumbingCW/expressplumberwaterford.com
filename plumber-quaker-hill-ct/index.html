A village in the northeast corner of Waterford

Quaker Hill is a village in the northeastern corner of Waterford, on the west bank of the Thames River north of New London. Its center is where Old Norwich Road meets Old Colchester Road. The name goes back to the late 1600s and the Rogerenes, a religious group tied to the area.

This page is for homeowners and small businesses in and around Quaker Hill. Call with your street address and a short description of the problem, and we will tell you what the next step looks like.

What the area looks like

The village center lies within the Quaker Hill Historic District, which is listed on the National Register of Historic Places and runs along Old Norwich Road from Thames View north to Route 32. Quaker Hill Elementary School is at 285 Bloomingdale Road, and the Quaker Hill Fire Company is on Old Colchester Road.

The 06375 ZIP code uses the Quaker Hill name but covers more than the village. It takes in the wider northeastern part of Waterford, including Bartlett, Best View, Cohanzie, Harrisons and Thames View. Give us your street address on the call so we know exactly where you are.

Public sewer or septic: check before you call

Waterford's Utility Commission also acts as the town's sewer authority, and its website links to a GIS map where you can look up your property. We cannot tell from here whether a given Quaker Hill street is on public sewer, so use that map or ask the Utility Commission office.

The answer matters when a drain backs up. A blockage in a house line is a job for drain cleaning, while a full septic tank or a failed leach field is a different problem. A camera look, described on our sewer inspection page, shows which one you have.

Older and newer houses

Quaker Hill has a historic district at its center, and houses in a village like this can range widely in age. Age alone does not tell you what is inside the walls.

A plumber can identify the pipe material at the meter, the main shutoff and under the sinks, then tell you whether anything needs work. If a repair turns into a larger project, see repiping for how that decision is made.

Permits and who to ask

Replacing a water heater, moving a drain or adding a fixture usually needs a permit. The Waterford Building Department, 15 Rope Ferry Road, accepts applications in person or through the CITY SQUARED portal. Ask the department before work starts if you are unsure whether your job needs one.

Code reference

According to the Connecticut Office of the State Building Inspector, the 2022 Connecticut State Building Code adopts the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC) for permit applications filed from October 1, 2022. A repair in this part of Waterford is judged by the same code as one anywhere else in the state.
