Bathtub Faucet Leak Repair in Waterford, CT Homes

A dripping tub spout or a wet wall behind the handle usually points to a worn cartridge or a failing seat. Here is how to sort it out.

☎ Call (860) 397-8103See Services

By the Waterford Express Plumbing team · Published 2026-09-30 · Updated 2026-11-16

Start with where the water appears

A bathtub faucet leak can show up at the spout, around the handle, at the showerhead or in the wall behind the tub. Where the water appears tells you what failed. A drip at the spout is different from a stain on the ceiling below.

This guide explains the common causes, what you can check yourself and when to call a plumber, especially if water is reaching walls or ceilings.

Match the symptom to the cause

A quick map for a tub and shower valve.

SymptomLikely causeNext step
Drip from spoutWorn cartridge or seatReplace cartridge.
Leak at the handleWorn O-ring or packingReplace seals.
Shower runs after shutoffDiverter or cartridgeReplace diverter parts.
Wet wall or ceiling belowSupply or valve leak behind the wallCall a plumber now.

What you can check yourself

Do this with the water off.

  1. Close the shutoff valves for the bathroom or the main.
  2. Remove the handle and trim, noting how the parts fit.
  3. Look for corrosion, scale and a worn cartridge.
  4. Take the old cartridge to a supplier to match it.
  5. Reassemble, turn the water on slowly and test.

When a repair is not a DIY job

If the valve body is corroded, the shutoff valves do not close or water appears on the ceiling below the tub, stop. These problems need a plumber because the repair may involve opening a wall and soldering or crimping new connections.

A leak inside the wall can rot framing and grow mold over time. Our faucet repair service handles the valve, and leak detection helps when you cannot see the source.

Preventing the next tub leak

Small habits extend the life of a tub valve.

  • Turn handles gently and avoid forcing them past the stop.
  • Clean the showerhead and spout of mineral buildup each season.
  • Check the caulk around the tub and tile, since gaps let water behind the wall.
  • Know where the shutoff for the bathroom is.
  • Replace a dripping cartridge soon, before the seat is damaged.
  • Look for stains on the ceiling or wall near the tub every few months.

How to tell where water is coming from

Dry the tub and tile completely, then run the water and watch. If water appears only when the shower runs, the leak may be at the showerhead arm or the wall fitting. If it shows when the tub fills, look at the spout and overflow. A moisture stain on the ceiling below suggests a supply or drain leak under the tub.

If you cannot tell, a plumber can pressure test the lines or use a moisture meter. Finding the source before opening a wall saves tile and time. Call us if you see swelling in drywall or soft spots in the floor.

Why cartridges and seats wear out

Every time you turn the handle, a cartridge or washer moves against a seat. Over years the rubber hardens, the brass pits and minerals build up. Water then slips past the seal, which shows as a drip at the spout.

Replacing the cartridge is quick and restores the seal in most cases. If the seat in a compression valve is pitted, it may need to be resurfaced or the valve replaced.

Shower diverter and tub spout problems

A tub with a shower should send all the water up when the diverter is pulled. If water keeps running from the spout, the diverter is worn or the spout needs to be replaced. A spout is often a simple twist off or set screw swap, and a new one costs little.

If the shower pressure feels weak after a repair, check the showerhead for mineral buildup before assuming a bigger problem. Soaking the head in vinegar often brings it back.

Repair or replace the valve

Single handle cartridges are a quick fix. A tub valve that is decades old, with parts no longer made, is better replaced. Replacement means opening the wall from the back or the tile side, so plan for patching and tile repair.

If you are remodeling the bathroom, replace the valve at the same time as the tile. It is cheaper to do it once than to open the wall again in a few years.

Waterford note: water quality and your fixtures

Waterford water comes from the New London system, which reports phosphate added for corrosion control and lime added for pH. The report lists lead and copper sampling results that stayed under the action levels in 2023.

That treatment protects the water in the pipes, but it does not change the age of your tub valve. If your house has old galvanized or lead pipe, the town explains a magnet and scratch test at the service line, and you can ask for help if you find something suspicious.

Hardness is not listed in the report, so test your tap if you see scale on the handle. Scale shortens the life of cartridges and is a common reason a faucet drips again.

According to the New London system's annual report, 2023 lead and copper sampling stayed below the EPA action levels, which is useful context before you decide how much of a bathroom to replace.
