Tankless Water Heater Tax Credit Status for Waterford, CT Homes

The federal home energy credit that once helped with tankless water heaters has ended. Here is what that means and where to look next.

☎ Call (860) 397-8103See Services

By the Waterford Express Plumbing team · Published 2026-10-12 · Updated 2026-11-16

The short answer for 2026

The federal Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit, often called 25C, ended for property placed in service after December 31, 2025. That means a tankless water heater installed in 2026 or later does not qualify under that credit, whatever older articles still say.

Search results often show outdated pages that describe the credit as active. Check IRS guidance or a tax professional before you count on any federal credit for a project this year, and remember we are plumbers, not tax advisors.

What the credit used to cover

Before it ended, the credit applied to certain efficient home improvements, including some qualifying water heaters, up to annual limits. Equipment had to meet specific efficiency standards, and the credit was claimed on a tax return.

If you installed a qualifying unit in 2025, you may still be able to claim the credit on that year's return. Keep your invoice and the maker's certification statement, and ask a tax professional to confirm eligibility.

For a unit installed in 2026, assume the federal credit is not available unless the IRS or Congress says otherwise. Plan your budget on that basis.

Where to look for other help

Programs change often, so verify each one.

  • Your electric or gas utility, which may offer rebates on efficient equipment.
  • The state energy efficiency program, which lists current incentives.
  • Manufacturer rebates and seasonal promotions.
  • Local or municipal programs, if any are offered.
  • Financing options offered by your installer or bank.

Understanding efficiency ratings

Water heaters carry an efficiency rating that tells you how much of the energy becomes hot water. A higher rating means lower operating cost over time, which matters more now that a federal credit is gone.

Compare the rating with the price and the expected life. A unit that costs more but runs more efficiently may pay for itself in energy savings, while a cheaper one may suit a household with low demand.

Timing a replacement

The best time to replace a heater is before it fails. Look for rust, moisture at the base, rumbling noises and a lack of hot water. If you see any of these, plan ahead.

An emergency replacement usually limits your choices to what is in stock. A planned one lets you compare models, check rebates and schedule the work at a convenient time.

Check your records before you file

If you installed a qualifying unit in 2025, keep the invoice, the model number and the manufacturer certification statement. Your tax preparer will ask for them. Having the paperwork in one folder saves time in spring.

Do not rely on a contractor's advice about tax eligibility. We can describe the equipment and the work, but eligibility is a tax question that depends on your situation.

Budgeting for a water heater replacement

Plan for the unit, the labor, any code related upgrades and the permit. Older homes may need new venting, a gas line change or a new expansion tank. These add cost but also add safety.

If your heater is more than a decade old, set aside money now. A planned replacement is cheaper and calmer than an emergency swap on a Sunday night. Our water heater replacement page explains how a replacement visit works.

Is a tankless unit still worth it

Without a credit, the case for tankless rests on space, continuous hot water and lower standby loss. If those matter to you, the unit may be worth the higher price.

If you mainly want the lowest first cost, a new tank heater is simpler. We explain both on our tankless and replacement pages, and water heater installation covers the work.

Waterford note: what to verify locally

Waterford's Building Department says permits are required before improvements, and incentive programs often ask for proof of a permitted, inspected installation. Ask the department what your project needs.

The New London system supplies Waterford water, and its annual report does not list hardness. Scale can reduce the life of a tankless heater, so a water test belongs in your decision whether or not a rebate is involved.

For rebate programs tied to your utility, confirm eligibility on the utility's own website, since terms change from year to year.

According to IRS guidance on the Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit, the credit applies to property placed in service through the end of 2025, which is why this page treats it as closed.
