Whole House Repipe with PEX in Waterford, CT

PEX is a flexible plastic pipe used for new supply lines. Here is how a repipe works and when it is the right move.

☎ Call (860) 397-8103See Services

By the Waterford Express Plumbing team · Published 2026-10-24 · Updated 2026-11-16

What repiping with PEX means

A whole house repipe replaces the supply piping in a home. PEX is a flexible plastic tubing that can be routed through walls with fewer joints than rigid pipe, which often means smaller openings and a faster project.

A repipe is a major job, so it makes sense when leaks keep returning, the water is discolored or the old pipes are known to fail. For a single leak, repair is usually enough.

Is repiping the right move

Signs that point toward a repipe.

  • Pinhole leaks in more than one place.
  • Low pressure that has gotten worse.
  • Rust colored water that clears slowly.
  • Galvanized or polybutylene supply lines.
  • A remodel that already has walls open.

PEX compared with copper

Both work. The choice depends on the home.

FactorPEXCopper
InstallationFlexible, fewer fittingsMore labor, soldered joints.
Wall openingsFewerMore.
Sun exposureMust be protectedNo concern.
Chemical sensitivitySome contaminants can affect itLess affected.

How a project runs

A repipe is a planned sequence.

  1. Inspect the existing lines and plan the route.
  2. Agree on a written scope and price.
  3. Shut off water in sections and install new lines.
  4. Patch small access openings and test every joint.
  5. Provide a closeout summary and handle the permit.

Signs your old pipes are near the end

Pipes rarely fail without warning. Look for a rise in water bills, stains on ceilings, a metallic taste or flakes in the aerator. Pressure that drops when two fixtures run together often points to narrowed pipes.

Galvanized steel corrodes from the inside and narrows over time. A pipe that looks fine on the outside can be nearly closed within. If you have it, a repipe may be a matter of when, not if.

Permits, inspections and your insurer

A repipe is a major plumbing project, so expect a permit and an inspection. Ask your plumber who pulls the permit and what the inspector checks, such as pressure tests and support spacing.

Tell your insurance agent about the project. Some insurers charge less for homes with new supply lines, and others ask about the age of plumbing on renewal. Keep the invoice and the permit paperwork with your records.

Questions to ask before you sign

A good plumber welcomes these.

  • What material and manifold layout will you use?
  • How will you protect floors and furniture?
  • How long will the water be off each day?
  • Who patches the walls, and who paints?
  • What is the warranty on the pipe and the labor?
  • Is the permit included in the price?

What the price depends on

Repipe quotes vary widely for good reasons.

  • The size of the house and the number of fixtures.
  • How hard the walls and ceilings are to reach.
  • The manifold or branch layout you choose.
  • Whether the water service line is replaced as well.
  • Wall repair and paint, which may be a separate trade.
  • Permit and inspection fees.

Manifold or branch layout

A manifold layout runs a separate line from a central point to each fixture. It gives steady pressure and lets you shut off one fixture at a time, but it uses more tubing. A branch layout uses trunk lines with smaller branches and is closer to traditional plumbing.

Ask your plumber which layout suits your house. Either can be done well, and the choice depends on the floor plan and your priorities.

Planning for a smooth repipe

Clear access to walls, closets and the basement before the crew arrives. Move furniture and valuables away from work areas, and plan for short periods without water. If you work from home, tell the crew so they can schedule noisy work around your calls.

Afterward, check the pressure at each fixture and look for leaks over the next few days. A good plumber returns if something needs adjusting.

Living in the house during the work

Most families stay home during a repipe. We plan the water shutoffs for short blocks and restore service at the end of the day when possible.

Expect dust and noise. We cover floors and furniture and clean up each day. Tell us about pets, home offices and anyone with medical needs, so we can plan around them. See our page on repiping for more.

Waterford note: confirm your pipe and the service line

The Waterford Utility Commission describes how to check your service line material near the meter with a magnet and a scratch test, and it keeps a service line material inventory. Plastic is not magnetic and blue, white or black, while galvanized is magnetic and hard to scratch.

The town is also replacing plastic water service laterals with copper under certain roads in 2026, at no direct cost to residents on those streets. If your street is on the list, coordinate your repipe with the timing so the new service connects to your new lines.

The Building Department says permits are required before improvements, and a repipe is a major project. Ask what applies and who pulls the permit. Our water service line repair page covers the outdoor section.

According to the Waterford Utility Commission, plastic is not magnetic and galvanized steel is, so the magnet test it describes is a quick first step before any repipe quote.
