Water Leak Sensors for Waterford, CT Homes

A small sensor on the floor can warn you of a leak before it ruins a basement. Here is how to choose, place and use them.

☎ Call (860) 397-8103See Services

By the Waterford Express Plumbing team · Published 2026-09-26 · Updated 2026-11-16

What a leak sensor does

A water leak sensor is a small device that sits on the floor and alerts you when water touches its contacts. Some sound a local alarm. Others send a message to your phone, which helps if the leak happens while you are away.

Sensors do not stop a leak, and they only detect water where they sit. They are most useful as an early warning near the places where water is most likely to appear.

Where sensors earn their keep

Place them where a slow leak is most likely to show first.

  • Beside the water heater and under the expansion tank.
  • Under kitchen and bathroom sinks.
  • Behind the washing machine.
  • Near a sump pit, with the sensor above the normal water level.
  • At the lowest corner of a finished basement.
  • Under a refrigerator with a water line.

Types of sensors

Pick the type that matches the risk.

What sensors cannot do

A sensor tells you water is present, not where the pipe has failed. A leak inside a wall may not reach the floor for a long time, so a quiet sensor is not proof that all is well.

Batteries need checking, and a Wi-Fi sensor needs a working network. Test each device when you install it and again each season, so you know the alert works.

If a sensor goes off, shut the main valve first, then call. Our leak detection service finds the source with equipment when the leak is hidden, and burst pipe repair covers the fix.

Maintaining your sensors

Test each sensor twice a year by touching the probe to a damp cloth. Replace batteries on the maker's schedule, and write the date on the device. A sensor that has gone silent is worse than none, since you assume you are protected.

Wipe dust from the contacts and check that the sensor still sits flat on the floor. If a pet or a vacuum moved it, put it back in place. Write down where each one sits, so you can check them quickly.

Pairing sensors with a shutoff plan

A sensor warns you, but you still have to act. Make sure every adult in the house knows where the main valve is and how to close it. Label it with a tag and keep the path to it clear.

If you are away often, a motorized shutoff valve tied to your sensors closes the line without you. We install them as part of plumbing installation projects and explain how to test them.

Choosing between sensor types

If someone is usually home, a battery alarm is enough. If you travel or keep a second home, a Wi-Fi sensor that texts your phone is worth the small extra cost. For a washing machine in a finished space or a water heater above a bedroom, a sensor tied to an automatic shutoff valve gives the best protection.

Look for a sensor with a long battery life, a loud alarm and a probe that sits flat on the floor. Cheap units that stop reporting after a few months are a common complaint, so test them on a schedule.

What to do when the alarm sounds

Move fast but stay safe.

  1. Check for water near the sensor and shut off the nearest valve.
  2. If you cannot tell the source, close the main water valve.
  3. Switch off power to wet areas if water is near outlets.
  4. Photograph the area for your insurer and mop up what you can.
  5. Call a plumber to find and fix the source.

Hidden leaks sensors miss

A pipe leaking inside a wall or under a slab may never reach a sensor on the floor. A water bill that climbs without a reason, a musty smell or a warm spot on the floor are clues that need a closer look.

That is where professional leak detection helps. A plumber listens, uses moisture tools and checks the meter. Repairing a hidden leak early avoids bigger damage later.

Waterford note: use your meter as a second check

The Waterford Utility Commission serves town water customers and can help with questions about meters and unexpected bills. A simple home test is to turn off every fixture and watch the meter. If it moves, water is going somewhere.

The town also describes a service line material check using a magnet and a scratch test at the pipe near the meter, and it is replacing plastic service laterals under some roads before paving in 2026. A leak outside the house on the utility side is theirs to repair, so call the Commission first.

Put a sensor near the point where the line enters your house, since that is where a service line problem often shows up first indoors.

According to the Waterford Utility Commission's own guidance, the magnet and scratch test is a simple way to learn your service line material, and the inventory it keeps feeds into federal lead rules.

Setting up a simple system

A weekend is enough to cover the main risks.

  1. Find your main shutoff and test that it closes fully.
  2. Place a sensor at each high risk spot from the list above.
  3. Connect Wi-Fi sensors to your phone and test each alert.
  4. Write the date and battery type on the device.
  5. Set a reminder to test them each season.
