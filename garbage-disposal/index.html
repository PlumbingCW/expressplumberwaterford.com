When the disposal stops working

Emergency garbage disposal repair and installation in Waterford, CT usually starts with a disposal that is humming and not turning, leaking under the sink or backing up into the dishwasher. Our 24/7 line can dispatch a plumber when it cannot wait.

Many disposal problems are simple, such as a jam cleared with a hex key or a reset button. Others point to a failed seal or motor, where replacement is the sensible option.

Leaking or backing up

Water under the sink or in the dishwasher is a sign to stop using both appliances.

Common disposal problems

A disposal that hums but does not spin is jammed. Turning the motor shaft with a hex key from underneath often frees it, after power is off. If the unit is dead, a reset button on the bottom may have tripped.

A leak at the top flange is a loose mounting ring or putty seal. A leak from the body usually means the internal seal has failed, and the unit must be replaced.

If water backs up from the disposal when the dishwasher runs, the drain hose or the sink drain may be partly blocked. In that case, the problem may be in the kitchen drain line.

A new disposal is a good time to check the dishwasher air gap, the drain trap and the electrical connection.

Disposal leaking?

Call the 24/7 line. For a slow kitchen drain, ask about drain cleaning, and see faucet repair for related sink fixtures.

Choosing a replacement disposal

A mid-size motor handles most households. Continuous-feed models are the norm, while batch-feed models run only when the stopper is in place and suit homes with small children.

Look at the warranty and the quality of the grinding chamber, not only horsepower. A higher-quality unit with sound insulation is easier to live with in an open kitchen.

What to do before the plumber arrives

Turn off the disposal switch and the breaker if you can reach it. Never put your hand in the chamber, even when the unit looks dead, because a stuck flywheel can move when freed.

If water is standing in the sink, stop running the faucet and bail out what you can. Take a photo of any visible leak under the cabinet, since it helps us bring the right parts.

Habits that keep a disposal running

Run cold water before, during and for a short time after each use so fats move through the trap arm. Feed scraps in small amounts, not a full load at once.

Avoid grease, coffee grounds in large amounts, fibrous peels, pasta and rice, which swell and pack the line. A disposal is meant for soft scraps, not as a second trash can, and a drain that backs up after use usually points to the line beyond the unit.

Code and permit note

A disposal swap is simple, while a first time install touches electrical and drain rules. The Connecticut State Building Code adopts the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), which covers the trap and drain arrangement, and a new circuit may need an electrical permit from the town.

Kitchen drains and disposal habits

Fibrous scraps, grease and starchy peels tend to clump in drains. The Waterford Utility Commission runs a fats, oil and grease program, and the advice it gives about keeping grease out of the sink applies to a home with a disposal as much as to a restaurant.

If you are unsure whether to install a disposal, note that homes on private septic should discuss it with a septic professional first, because extra solids add load to the tank.
