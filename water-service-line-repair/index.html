The pipe nobody sees

Emergency water service line repair in Waterford, CT is for the pipe that carries water from the street main to your house. When it leaks, the signs are a wet patch in the yard, a meter that runs with the house shut off, low pressure or a surprise water bill.

This line is buried, so repairs involve digging, and good planning matters. We locate the leak first, so the excavation is as small as possible, and we explain who is responsible for which section.

A service line leak: what to do

An active service line leak can undermine soil and flood a basement.

Waterford's plastic service line project

The Waterford Utility Commission and the Department of Public Works are replacing plastic water service laterals with copper tubing under several roads in 2026, before the roads are resurfaced. The town says crews check the pipe material by digging a small hole at the property line and notifies residents individually if their lateral needs replacement, at no direct cost to residents.

If you hear about road work on your street, ask the Commission whether your service is included. If your own pipe is plastic and not part of the project, or if the failure is on your side of the property line, a private repair is a separate matter, and we can help.

The Commission also runs a service line material inventory and describes a simple check using a magnet and a scratch test. Plastic is blue, white or black and not magnetic. Galvanized is magnetic and hard to scratch. Lead scratches easily and is not magnetic.

Service line repairs depend on the route and the pipe.

  • Pipe material, depth and length of the damaged section.

  • Locating the leak first to limit digging.
  • Permits or utility coordination.
  • Insurance note: some homeowner policies offer an optional service line endorsement, so check whether you added one.

Signs of a service line problem

Not every leak shows up as a puddle.

  • Water meter turns when every fixture is off.
  • A sudden rise in the water bill.
  • Low pressure throughout the house.
  • A soft or sunken patch in the lawn or driveway.
  • Discolored water after the line was disturbed.

Planning the dig

Before any excavation we call for utility marking so we do not strike gas, electric or communication lines. We protect lawns and walkways where we can and restore the surface after the repair.

Trenchless methods can reduce digging in some soils, but not every pipe qualifies. We explain the choice on site and give you a price before the work starts.

Code and permit note

The service line is regulated twice, by the utility for the street side and by code for your side. The Connecticut State Building Code adopts the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), and the Waterford Utility Commission says how it wants connections made. Ask both before excavation starts.

Pipe materials and lead

Older service lines can be galvanized steel, which corrodes from the inside, or lead, which the EPA requires utilities to inventory. If you suspect lead, the Waterford Utility Commission asks homeowners to report it and will provide replacement information.

The City of New London Utilities annual water quality report, which covers Waterford, lists lead and copper sampling at the tap and describes phosphate added for corrosion control. That is useful background, but it does not tell you what your own pipe is made of, so check it.
