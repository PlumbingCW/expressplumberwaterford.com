Why a shoreline village needs a plan

Houses close to the water often have crawlspaces and low basements, which is where leaks and backups do the most harm. A slow drain in a low bathroom or a damp smell in a utility room deserves a quick look before it becomes a flooded floor.

If your Niantic home sits empty for part of the year, shut off the main water supply when you leave and drain exposed lines before a cold snap. A frozen pipe in a closed house can split silently and release water for days. See burst pipe repair for what we do when that happens.

For a home that is lived in all year, the best habit is simple. Know where your main shutoff is, and test it once a year so it turns when you need it.

Water in East Lyme homes

Niantic is served by the East Lyme Water and Sewer Commission, whose annual report says customers draw from seven groundwater wells and that the system measured hardness of roughly 21 to 50 ppm as calcium carbonate. That is soft water, so scale is less of a worry than it is in many towns, but the same report lists manganese and iron treatment, which can leave staining on fixtures.

Staining at a tub or a toilet bowl is a cosmetic issue and not a plumbing failure. If your water suddenly turns brown or tastes metallic, call the Commission first, because a main break or hydrant flushing nearby is the usual cause. If it happens only inside your house, call us.

The Commission also supplements supply in summer with water from New London, which means pressure and taste can change with the season. A pressure change that affects one fixture is a plumbing problem, while a change at every tap is worth a call to the utility.

Code reference for Niantic

East Lyme's Building Department works from the same state code as every Connecticut town. According to the Office of the State Building Inspector, the 2022 Connecticut State Building Code adopts the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), so a repair in Niantic is judged by the same rules as one in Waterford.

Permits and who to ask

East Lyme states that interior remodels and structural changes need a building permit and that its Building Department sits within the Land Use offices at Town Hall, 108 Pennsylvania Avenue in Niantic. Ask there if your project goes beyond a simple repair.
