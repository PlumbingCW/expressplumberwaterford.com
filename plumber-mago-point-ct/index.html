A working waterfront village

Mago Point is a waterfront village on the Niantic River. Waterford's plan calls it the town's only active commercial waterfront, with small lots, water access and views toward Oswegatchie Hill and Niantic Bay.

This is a mixed district rather than a pure residential subdivision. This page covers both the homes and the businesses, so tell us which you are when you call.

What the zoning says

The town created a Village District here, with its own design guidelines. The district names Mago Point Way as the primary street, and Second Street, Avenue B, River Street and Niantic River Road as secondary streets.

Mixed uses are allowed. That includes retail, restaurants, cafes, offices, hotels and bed and breakfasts, as well as boat docks, boat sales and service, and fishing and lobstering operations. Existing homes are permitted, and multifamily housing is allowed on secondary and tertiary streets where no business is proposed.

Restaurants and food businesses

A kitchen on a small waterfront lot has the same basic needs as any other, and a few habits help.

  • Scrape plates and wipe pans before washing so fats stay out of the drain.
  • Ask a plumber whether the kitchen needs a grease trap or interceptor and how often it should be cleaned.
  • Schedule line cleaning before the busy season, not after a backup.
  • Know where the main water shutoff and the floor drains are.
  • Ask about work outside service hours so a repair does not close the kitchen.

Homes and rental properties

Some buildings in the district mix a business on the ground floor with housing above, which the zoning permits. Plumbing in a mixed building often serves both uses, so repairs need a clear picture of what runs where.

For a planned fixture upgrade, see plumbing installation. For a drain that keeps slowing down, see hydro jetting, and for business calls, see commercial plumbing.

Near the water

Buildings near a river can have low spaces that are hard to dry once wet. That is a general point about low spaces and not a statement about any one building in Mago Point.

If your building has a sump pit or floor drain, ask about sump pump service and what protects the space during heavy rain.

Permits and who to ask

Replacing a water heater, moving a drain or adding a fixture usually needs a permit. The Waterford Building Department, 15 Rope Ferry Road, accepts applications in person or through the CITY SQUARED portal. New construction and exterior renovations in the Village District are reviewed against its design guidelines, so mention the district to the town if your project changes the outside of a building. Ask the department before work starts if you are unsure whether your job needs one.

Code reference

According to the Connecticut Office of the State Building Inspector, the 2022 Connecticut State Building Code adopts the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC) for permit applications filed from October 1, 2022. A repair in this part of Waterford is judged by the same code as one anywhere else in the state.
