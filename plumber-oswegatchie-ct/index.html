A shoreline village on a sandy point

Oswegatchie sits on a sandy point on Waterford's western border, between the mouth of the Niantic River and Keeney Cove. Boston Post Road is the main way in. Its streets include East Street, Riverside Drive, Plant Drive, Park Drive, Shawandassee Road and Oswegatchie Road.

A National Register nomination describes it as a former summer resort colony that later became a year-round community. Call us with your address and what is happening, and we will help you plan the next step.

What the record says about the neighborhood

The nomination counts 51 parcels in the historic district, 50 of them residential and one a church. Most significant houses sit along the water on large lots. The earliest surviving houses date to the 1870s and 1880s, most contributing buildings date from about 1900 to 1920, and some ranch and modern homes were added after 1950.

The district is only part of the wider Oswegatchie area, so the nomination should not be read as a description of every home nearby. Ask us about your own house, and we will look at what is actually there.

If the house is used part of the year

A house that was built as a summer home, or is opened only for part of the year, is easier to protect with a routine.

  • Know where the main water valve is and that it closes fully.
  • Ask a plumber about draining the lines and the water heater before a long absence.
  • After reopening, turn the water on slowly and check under sinks and around the heater for drips.
  • Look at the ceiling below any upstairs bathroom for new stains.
  • Test the sump pump or floor drain if the house has one.

Near the water: what to ask

Homes near a river or cove may have low basements or crawlspaces. A leak or backup does the most harm there. That is a general point about low spaces, not a claim about any one Oswegatchie house.

If you have a floor drain or a sump pit, see sump pump service and ask what protects the space during heavy rain. For a slow drain upstairs or down, start with drain cleaning.

Hot water for a house with changing use

A water heater that sits unused for months can develop trouble that shows up on the first cold morning. If the tank is old, leaking or slow to heat, read about repair and replacement before deciding.

Tell us the fuel type and where the tank sits, and we can say what the work involves.

Permits and who to ask

Replacing a water heater, moving a drain or adding a fixture usually needs a permit. The Waterford Building Department, 15 Rope Ferry Road, accepts applications in person or through the CITY SQUARED portal. Ask the department before work starts if you are unsure whether your job needs one.

Code reference

According to the Connecticut Office of the State Building Inspector, the 2022 Connecticut State Building Code adopts the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC) for permit applications filed from October 1, 2022. A repair in this part of Waterford is judged by the same code as one anywhere else in the state.
