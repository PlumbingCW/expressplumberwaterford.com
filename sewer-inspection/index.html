Seeing inside the pipe

A sewer inspection in Waterford, CT puts a waterproof camera on a flexible cable and sends it through your lateral while you watch a monitor. It shows roots, cracks, offsets, bellies and buildup that no one can judge from the surface.

This is a planned service, useful before a home purchase, after repeated clogs or before a remodel. If sewage is already backing up now, call about sewer line cleaning or sewer line repair.

When an inspection makes sense

Buying a house is the most common reason. A line that looks fine from the cleanout can have a crack twenty feet out, and the repair cost then becomes the buyer's problem. An inspection before closing gives you the information to negotiate.

Repeated slow drains, gurgling fixtures and recurring clogs are the next reason. A camera shows whether the cause is grease, roots or a physical defect, so you spend on the right fix.

Homeowners planning a remodel, a new bath or a basement project also benefit. Adding fixtures puts more load on the lateral, and knowing its condition prevents surprises.

What happens during the visit

The process is straightforward and you can watch it live.

  1. Locate the cleanout or access point, or pull a toilet if none exists.
  2. Feed the camera through the line while recording.
  3. Mark the depth and location of any defect with a locator.
  4. Review the footage with you on the monitor and in plain terms.
  5. Provide a written summary of findings and options.

What changes the price

The price depends on access and what we find.

  • Whether a cleanout exists or one needs to be installed.
  • Line length and the number of branches to check.
  • Cleaning before the camera if the pipe is blocked.
  • Locating the defect on the surface with a tracer.
  • Insurance note: inspections are usually a homeowner expense, though a home inspection report may reference the findings.

Reading the report you receive

The summary lists each defect with its distance from the cleanout, the pipe material and a recommendation. Distances matter, because a crack at fifteen feet is a different job from one at sixty feet under a driveway.

If the report is clean, keep it with your house records. If it shows defects, ask for a ranked list: what needs fixing now, what can wait and what is only worth watching. That keeps you from paying for work that is not urgent.

When an inspection is worth booking

Camera work is useful in these moments.

  • Before buying a house with an older sewer line.
  • After repeated backups or slow drains.
  • Before a basement remodel that adds a bathroom.
  • When trees stand over the lateral.
  • After construction work near the line.

Code and permit note

An inspection is observation, not construction, so no permit is involved. What you do with the findings may be regulated, because the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC) adopted by Connecticut sets the rules for drain slope and materials in any repair that follows.

Sewer lines in Waterford

Waterford has both public sewer and private septic. The Waterford Utility Commission manages the public system. If your house sits on the sewer, the camera should run from your cleanout to the connection point at the main. If your house is on septic, the camera covers the line from the house to the tank, and the tank itself is inspected separately.

If the inspection finds a defect in the town main, report it to the Waterford Utility Commission, and keep the video. Having footage speeds up the conversation about who handles the fix.
