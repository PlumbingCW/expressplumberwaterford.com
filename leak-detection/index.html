Finding the leak you cannot see

Emergency leak detection in Waterford, CT starts with a symptom, not a pipe. A water bill that jumped, a meter that spins with every tap off, a stain on a ceiling or a musty smell in a closet are all common ways a leak announces itself.

Our job is to trace the water to its source with minimal damage. That means listening equipment, thermal imaging and pressure testing, followed by a clear explanation of what was found and what repair is needed.

When a leak is an emergency

Water coming through a ceiling, spreading under a wall or running behind a finished surface needs fast action.

If your meter keeps moving

Turn off every tap and appliance, then check your water meter. If the dial or low-flow indicator keeps moving, water is going somewhere. That could be a leaking toilet flapper, a failed irrigation valve, a slab leak or a break in the service line.

Homes on public water have a meter, and the Waterford Utility Commission, handles the system. If the meter moves after you have shut the house valve, the leak is likely between the meter and the house, which is a water service line issue.

A sudden high bill from a hidden leak is common in older homes along Waterford's older roads and villages, such as Jordan and Quaker Hill, where supply lines may be galvanized or copper that has aged.

Where leaks hide in Waterford homes

These are the places we check first.

  • Under-house crawlspaces and basements near the main entry.
  • Behind tubs and showers, where drain and supply lines meet.
  • Ceilings below upstairs baths and kitchens.
  • Water heater and boiler connections.
  • Irrigation lines and outdoor spigots, especially after winter.
  • Underground service lines between the meter and the house.

Moisture is not always a leak

Condensation on cold pipes, a poorly vented bathroom and a window that does not shed water can all mimic a plumbing leak. Before we open anything, we check whether the moisture follows a pattern that matches those causes.

That step saves money. A thermal camera can show a cold, wet area, but only a pressure test or listening check confirms that water is escaping a pipe. We tell you honestly when a leak turns out to be something else.

Code and permit note

Finding a leak is diagnostic work, but fixing it may involve code. The 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), adopted by the Connecticut State Building Code, sets how pipes must be supported and protected, which matters when a repair opens a wall. Ask the town if a larger repair needs a permit.

Pool, spa, yard and meter box leaks

Not every leak is inside the house. A pool or spa that loses water faster than evaporation explains, a soft patch in the yard or a meter box that stays full of water can all point to a supply line problem outside the walls.

Start with the meter test, then tell us where you see the symptom. We track outdoor leaks by pressure testing and listening, which limits how much of the yard is dug up.

Water quality and your pipes

The City of New London Utilities annual report, which covers Waterford, notes phosphate added for corrosion control and lime added to adjust pH. Water chemistry cannot stop an old pipe from failing, though, and a pinhole leak often forms where a pipe has aged. If you see repeated pinholes, ask us about repiping.

Private well owners should test water annually per state health department guidance, since corrosive water can shorten pipe life.
