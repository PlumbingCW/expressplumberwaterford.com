Adding plumbing the right way

Plumbing installation in Waterford, CT covers new work: a bathroom added in a basement, a relocated kitchen sink, a laundry hookup or a new outdoor spigot. These are planned projects, so the key is a clear scope before pipes are cut.

The most common mistake is underestimating drains. A drain needs a slope toward the main line, a vent for air and a trap to block sewer gas. A fixture that looks simple on paper may need a lot of hidden work.

Planning a new bath or kitchen

Start with the drain path. If the fixture is below the main sewer line, as in many basements, a sewage ejector pump may be needed. That adds cost and a maintenance item, so we explain it early.

Next, check the supply. Adding a bath may require a larger supply line or a second water heater, particularly in a house with a small existing system.

Venting is the third piece. Each fixture needs a vent path to the roof or an approved air admittance valve where code allows it. The inspector will look at this, so it is worth settling before walls close.

Finally, choose fixtures before rough-in, because the rough-in dimensions depend on the specific toilet, vanity and shower valve you pick.

What we confirm before starting

These points keep a project on track.

  • Fixture list and rough-in dimensions.
  • Drain slope, venting and trap arrangement.
  • Water supply size and pressure.
  • Shutoff valves for every new fixture.
  • Permits and inspection steps.
  • Insurance note: remodel work is generally an owner cost, and a future leak from a new install may be covered, so keep paperwork.

Permits and licensing

New plumbing generally needs a permit. The Waterford Building Department, 15 Rope Ferry Road, accepts applications in person or through the CITY SQUARED portal and states that permits are required before new construction, additions, renovations or improvements. Rules on scope vary, so ask what your project needs.

Connecticut licenses plumbers through the Department of Consumer Protection. A homeowner can check a license on elicense.ct.gov. A licensed plumber who pulls the permit and arranges inspection protects you when you sell the house.

What changes the price

Installation prices depend on the project.

  • Number and type of fixtures.
  • Rough-in work in finished or unfinished space.
  • Drain and vent complexity.
  • Pumps or backwater valves if below grade.
  • Fixtures supplied by you or by us.
  • Phased work if you are living in the house.

Questions to ask any plumber before a project

A good plumber welcomes these questions.

  • Is the license active and who pulls the permit?
  • What is included at rough-in and at trim-out?
  • Who supplies fixtures and who handles returns?
  • How long will water be off and when?
  • What happens if we find hidden problems in the wall?

Protecting your finished space

Plan the layout with the plumber before tile or cabinets go in. Access panels behind a tub or washer valves are small details that make future repairs painless.

Keep a record of valve locations and fixture models. A one page sheet taped inside a cabinet door helps the next person who works on the house, including you.

Code and permit note

New plumbing is governed by the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), which the 2022 Connecticut State Building Code adopts according to the state's published code page. Drain slope, venting and fixture spacing are its core rules, and the Waterford Building Department reviews your project.

Coordinating with other trades

Plumbing sits in the middle of a remodel. We need walls open before the electrician finishes, and we need the framer to leave room for drains. A short call with your contractor before demolition saves expensive rework.

If you act as your own general contractor, ask us for a simple written scope that lists what is included at rough-in and what happens at trim-out when fixtures are set.
